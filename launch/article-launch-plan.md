# Making the article travel

X Articles get distribution from the post that carries them, not from the article itself. So the plan is
a post, a thread of replies that re-hook people who scroll past, and a second wave two days later.

## Hour 0
1. Publish the article with the before/after cover.
2. Carry post: option A from article.md. It leads with the 111K tweet because that's the shared memory
   everyone has. Don't link the original tweet in the main post; quote it in reply 2 instead (a quote
   tweet of a viral post gets shown to that post's audience).
3. Reply 1 (within 2 minutes): the six rules as a plain list, no images. This is the bookmark magnet.
   People bookmark rules, not products.
4. Reply 2: quote the Ryan Vogel tweet with "this one. every model. here's the fix ↓"
5. Reply 3: `images/one-prompt.png` and the install line.
6. Pin the carry post.

## Hour 1 to 6
- Reply to every reply. Replies in the first hour are the strongest signal X has.
- Anyone posting their own output: quote it with one honest compliment about their palette choice.
- Anyone saying "it's just radial gradients": agree, and paste the nine-layer CSS. Being generous with the
  trick makes people share it.

## Day 2
- Post `images/palettes.png` alone: "seven palettes, one skill, zero purple." Link the article in a reply.
- If a design-engineer account with reach replied, quote them.

## Day 4
- The wallpaper. "2560x1440, free, made with /gradient-skin." Wallpapers get saved and re-shared.

## What not to do
- No hashtags. No "🧵". No "excited to share".
- Don't edit the post after ten minutes.
- Don't post the article link naked. The card needs a sentence in front of it.
- Don't argue with anyone who says the good gradient is also a cliché. They're right. Reply "yes, but
  it's the good cliché" and move on.

## Timing
Tue to Thu. 8 to 10am Pacific reaches US design and dev, 6 to 8pm IST reaches India. Pick whichever
side your followers lean. If you can't decide, 9am Pacific is 9:30pm IST and catches both.
